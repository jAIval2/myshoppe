All glass.

Inside the disc

Smaller than the finest grind.

The filter is glass powder fused into a disc. The gaps between the grains are 16–40 µm (G3 / ISO P40). Water passes. Grounds stay on top. Glass does not absorb flavour, and it does not rust or tear.

  • GradeG3 / ISO 4793 P40, pores 16–40 µm
  • MaterialBorosilicate 3.3, like lab glassware
  • Honest limitVery fine particles (~20–50 µm) can lodge in the pores, so the disc needs a citric-acid soak from time to time.

Paper vs glass

Paper keeps the oils. Glass gives them back.

Paper filters trap most coffee oils. A glass disc lets them through, so the cup has more body, like a metal-filter brew, but clearer. Nothing in the brew path is plastic, paper or metal.

Your target: glass landed at ₹5,000 or less including GST and shipping, or ₹6,000–7,000 from premium lab makers.

Clean

Rinse with hot water after each brew. Soak in 2–5% citric acid weekly. Use IPA or ethanol for oil build-up.

Never

No hydrofluoric acid, no hot concentrated phosphoric acid, no hot alkali or dishwasher detergent on the disc.

Limits

Keep temperature changes under 100 K; no cold water on hot glass. The brew vacuum is mild (−0.2 to −0.3 bar); discs are rated to ~1 bar differential.

03 · Quality is the brand

Every disc gets a bubble-point test, and a number.

Wet the disc, then push air up from below. The pressure at which the first bubbles appear shows the largest pore: d ≈ 4γ / ΔP, so 4 × 0.072 N/m ÷ 7,000 Pa ≈ 41 µm.

Pass: first bubbles at 70–75 cm of water (~7 kPa). Fail: under ~35 cm, which means pores larger than 80 µm.

This is what separates a ₹20K brewer from a ₹1,800 lab funnel. Film it and print the result on a card in every box.

Test every supplier sample

  1. Oil immersion: confirms borosilicate.
  2. Bubble point: confirms the G3 grade.
  3. Flow time: 500 ml through a wet disc, compared with a reference unit.
  4. Thermal shock: hot water into the funnel at room temperature, within the 100 K limit.
  5. Visual and fit: even fusion, no chips or star cracks, the joints seat properly.

Let buyers set the price

Ask 20–30 people in roaster communities four questions: at what price is it too cheap to trust, a bargain, getting expensive, too expensive to consider?

Then run deposits at two prices, ₹17,999 and ₹19,999. If the ₹19,999 group puts down deposits at 70% or more of the ₹17,999 rate, the higher price earns more.

Two rules

Don't claim "permanent" or "never needs fixing". Glass breaks and discs clog. Say "no paper, no cloth, nothing to replace", and sell spare parts.

A launch price must be real. "Founding batch ₹17,999, then ₹19,999" is fine only if the price really goes up afterwards.

06 · Sales model

Build to order. No stock, no COD.

  1. Deposit₹3,000 to reserve a numbered unit; refundable until the batch order is placed.
  2. BatchOrder the glass once a batch fills, and bubble-point test every disc.
  3. BalancePaid before dispatch, delivered within 4–6 weeks. Prepaid only, no COD.
  4. UnboxingA QR code on the box opens a page where the buyer uploads an unboxing video. That video is the evidence for transit-damage claims, which you then replace for free.

Stack, at zero fixed cost

  1. SiteFree container host, behind a Cloudflare Tunnel to your laptop
  2. OrdersSupabase always takes the order, then syncs to your laptop's Postgres, with pg_dump backups. Note: Supabase's free tier pauses after 7 days of inactivity.
  3. PaymentsRazorpay: deposit link, then a balance invoice
  4. EmailBrevo or Resend; get a domain before launch

07 · Compliance

Sort these out before taking the first deposit.

Do

GST registration

Required before selling goods to other states, whatever your turnover. Choose regular registration, not the composition scheme, which bars inter-state sales.

Verify

BIS laboratory glassware order

Whether the order covers a brewer is unclear (IS 1381 scope is disputed). Get a written opinion. Don't count on the MSME relaxation; it is ending.

Verify

Hotplate ISI mark

Whether an ISI mark is mandatory is unconfirmed. Ship an ISI-marked hotplate anyway.

Do

Legal Metrology label

MRP, contents, maker or packer address, month and year, and a customer-care contact on every box.

Do

E-Commerce Rules 2020

Publish refund and return terms, a grievance officer and the country of origin. You can't refuse a refund for defective goods.

Don't

Dark patterns (2023)

No false urgency, no fake "was" price, no fees added at checkout.

Do

Food contact

Borosilicate 3.3 is food-safe. There is no named Indian Standard for glass food contact, so keep supplier material declarations on file.

Do

Original brand

Original name, art and photos only. No Kazumi marks or footage. Run a trademark search on the working name.

Do

Safety sheet

Instructions on heat, vacuum and handling. Tell buyers to inspect the glass for chips before each brew and to keep the brewer away from children.

08 · Brand and channels

The brand is the margin. Quality checks back it up.

The glass costs about ₹5K. The other ₹15K pays for trust, looks and the story, and the visible quality checks are what make that believable.

The show

A siphon is the most watchable way to make coffee: it boils, rises, then gets pulled down through glass. Every brew is a Reel.

Lab glass in the kitchen

Lean into the science: borosilicate, an ISO P40 disc, a numbered test card in the box.

One promise

"No paper. No cloth. Nothing to replace." Back it with spare parts that are always in stock.

Where the buyers already are

  • Specialty roasters: a bundle with one roaster's beans beats ten influencers.
  • Instagram coffee pages: micro creators (10–50K followers) charge ~₹5–25K per Reel; coffee enthusiasts often accept the product instead.
  • Reddit, Team-BHP's coffee thread, Discord and WhatsApp brewer groups: answer questions, don't advertise.
  • Brewers Cup and barista circles: one respected brewer's endorsement is enough.
  • Corporate and wedding gifting: a second channel at ₹20K.

What makes or breaks it

  1. You on camera. Accounts fronted by the founder sell niche hardware; faceless brand accounts don't.
  2. Content that teaches: why glass filters give a clearer cup, how to clean the disc, how to dial in a recipe.
  3. Consistency: 3 posts a week for 12 weeks matters more than one viral hit.
  4. Proof on film: the same beans through glass, paper and cloth, a blind tasting, and the bubble-point test.

10 · Risks and upside

Both sides, honestly.

Why it could fail

  • Evidence of demand in India is weak, and an unknown brand at ₹20K narrows it further.
  • Glass breaks in transit and in kitchens; that costs replacements and reviews.
  • Supplier quality varies; the ₹1,800 funnel is unproven until tested.
  • Unclear BIS and ISI rules could delay launch.
  • Content takes founder time every week for months.

What could go our way

  • No glass-filter siphon is sold in India that we could find.
  • Kazumi costs ₹26–38K landed; we're ₹6–18K cheaper with a heat source included.
  • The product creates its own content, and the coffee festival audience is large.
  • 47–59% margins on Showcase with no inventory risk, because every kit is built to order.
  • Spare parts, cleaning kits and roaster bundles add repeat revenue.
